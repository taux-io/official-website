{% call cover("01", "What is GEO", flush=true) %}What is GEO? {% endcall %}

GEO — Generative Engine Optimization is the practice of structuring content so it shows up, and gets cited, in what generative engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini) actually say.

The goal

To be the cited source inside an AI answer, and the one it recommends.

The difference

SEO targets keyword rankings. GEO targets how a model understands meaning and relates entities.

{% call cover("02", "Why GEO matters") %}Why it matters now{% endcall %}

Search habits changed

More people ask a question outright than type keywords.

Better-qualified traffic

Someone asking a specific question is usually closer to a decision.

Authority, demonstrated

When an AI quotes your position as the answer, that is the claim being made for you.

Reusable by construction

Content structured well for models turns out to be good social material too.

{% call cover("04", "GEO research, KDD 2024") %}Nine methods, measured: the KDD 2024 study{% endcall %}

A paper presented at KDD 2024 by researchers at Princeton University and IIT Delhi and two independent researchers, "GEO: Generative Engine Optimization" (Aggarwal et al.), tested nine ways of rewriting a web page and measured how much of a generative engine's answer each one earned the source. Eight raised it; one lowered it:

Method Change vs. baseline What it means in practice
Quotation addition +41% Add direct quotations from relevant people or experts, and say who said them.
Adding statistics +31% Replace vague wording with specific numbers — percentages, counts, durations.
Fluency optimization +28% Make the sentences read more smoothly and the paragraphs connect.
Citing sources +27% Back claims with external sources a reader can check.
Technical terms +18% Use the field's accepted technical terms where they fit.
Easy-to-understand +14% Rewrite complex ideas in plain, direct language.
Authoritative tone +10% Use a more assertive, persuasive tone.
Unique words +6% Vary the wording instead of repeating the same terms.
Keyword stuffing −8% Repeat the query's keywords on purpose. In this experiment it did worse than changing nothing.

The figures are this site's conversion of the paper's Table 1 into relative change: the overall Position-Adjusted Word Count (how much of the answer comes from the source, weighted by how early it appears) against the unmodified baseline of 19.3. The engine was GPT-3.5-turbo answering from the top five Google results, on the paper's own GEO-bench queries. Source: arXiv:2311.09735

What these numbers don't tell you

They are averages on one benchmark, not a promise. The paper itself notes that generative engines keep changing, so methods may need to change with them, and that the questions people ask change too; the effect also varies by domain. A check on Perplexity.ai pointed the same way, by different amounts. What was measured is a source's share of the answer — not clicks, enquiries or revenue.
